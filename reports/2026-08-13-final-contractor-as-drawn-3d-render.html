01 / Executive Summary

Executive Summary

What was delivered

Five text-less PDF sheets were turned into a compiled, check-passing building model (designs/contractor-as-drawn.json), a 12-view final render gallery, a 1.12 MiB GLB, and a self-contained web viewer — published to deliverables/contractor-as-drawn/.

Why it matters

The client family has so far seen only 2D sheets and a written review. This model depicts the building the contractor actually drew — a single-family seven-level house, not the brief's mixed-use five-level scheme — so the render carries evidentiary weight in the programme discussion that the review's finding C-01 opened.

Headline result

The model compiles clean — 0 schema errors and 0 check items — and all 12 renders are neutral (no miscolour), proven fresh against the model hash. The 12-view final gallery took 24.2 minutes on legacy EEVEE.

The honest caveat

This execution environment cannot read the sheet glyphs (no image input, no text layer), so dimensions trace to the already-measured review rather than a fresh read; per-room subdivision is flagged (inferred). Every departure from the drawing — skewed boundaries, glazed light-well cap, winder treads, enlarged stair shaft, plant room — is ledgered, not hidden.

02 / Background & Objective

Background & Objective

Five contractor sheets arrived in contractor/ — plans for the ground + mezzanine, floors 2–3–4, floor 5 + roof terrace, the roof + front elevation, and a single long section (A-A). They were reviewed on 2026-08-12 into a 13-finding register, but that review is prose and 2D crops. Nobody has seen the building the contractor actually drew, in three dimensions.

The objective was to encode that seven-level tube house (3.95 × 25 m, a ~7.2°-skewed plot, a stair-and-lift core that stacks through seven plates, a light well capped with glass, and a rooftop plant room) as a new spec that the homedesign pipeline can compile, check, render and export — and to publish the result as a family-facing artifact with a sidecar ledger recording every place the model departs from the sheet.

04 / Assumptions & Constraints

Assumptions & Constraints

Assumptions

  • Plot is the orthogonal collapse of the skewed rectangle: 3960 × 25000 mm; front yard 3000, rear yard 2000.
  • Seven storeys: trệt (3800) / lửng (3200) / tầng 2–5 (3400 ×4) / sân thượng (3200) — heights from the section tags.
  • The light well is expressed by omission (untiled footprint) with a roof void over it.
  • Winders become a u_return flight; risers re-derived per storey.

Constraints

  • Room type is a closed 12-value enum; site is a rectangle; roofs are axis-aligned.
  • One stair object per storey; check_room_support is a hard error (≥80% coverage).
  • Renders must run on Blender 4.1's legacy EEVEE (EEVEE Next renders blood-red on this iGPU).
  • Shadows are decorative — the schema has no north angle and the sheets have no north point.
05 / Methodology

Methodology

The work ran as five phases over the existing pipeline, with no change to how the pipeline models geometry. Phase 1 recorded every dimension with its source, cross-checked against the review's two-way calibration and the plan's binding defaults. Phase 2 authored the spec so it tiles every level exactly, stacks its core byte-identically, and satisfies the five-rule check registry — applying the stair fit (Blondel) up front so a compile failure surfaced as a finding rather than a crash. Phase 3 wrote the fidelity ledger (drawing → model → why → does the render change). Phase 4 built, rendered (legacy EEVEE final), and exported. Phase 5 published the finals.

One necessary deviation from "no code change": compiling a spec whose room names carry Vietnamese diacritics exposed a Windows encoding bug (files read/written with the CP1252 locale default instead of UTF-8), which mangled P.KHÁCH or raised. Fixed by pinning encoding="utf-8" at the file I/O sites — a correctness fix with no logic change.

07 / Findings & Recommendation

Findings & Recommendation

The model is faithful where it can be, and honest where it can't.

The measured facts — plot, storey heights, core, programme — are reproduced. Two things the schema cannot express had to be approximated and are therefore findings about the drawing, not just modelling compromises:

  • The stair shaft is too small as drawn. A 3005 × 3200 mm shaft cannot hold a Blondel U-return at a 3800 mm storey (needs ~3998 mm run). The model enlarges it to 3005 × 4000 mm — flagged as a candidate review finding (the drawing's copy-pasted stair block never resolves which riser each floor uses).
  • The light well is smaller than drawn (2200 × 1300 → 1160 × 1600 mm) to tile the lift band, and its glazed cap is modelled as an open void because the renderer has no glass.

The render also makes the programme discrepancy concrete: the contractor drew a single-family house (living + kitchen on the ground floor, bedrooms above, P.THỜ on tầng 5) where the brief asked for leased floors — which is exactly what the family needs to see to have the C-01 conversation.

Recommendation

Show the gallery and viewer to the family as a walkthrough, with one sentence pointing to the review report for the findings. Treat the enlarged stair shaft and the glazed light-well cap as open questions for the contractor alongside the existing 13 findings.

09 / Risks / Open Questions

Risks / Open Questions

Risks

  • Persuasiveness: a render is convincing in a way a caveat is not — the ledger ships beside the renders for exactly this reason.
  • Context: shown alone, the single-family scheme could be read as the agreed design; the published page links the review report.
  • Inference: per-room subdivision is inferred, not read off the sheet; a future pass with image reading should verify.

Open questions

  • Is the drawn light-well glazing fixed or operable, and how does the well exhaust (finding C-02)?
  • What is the intended lift technology — the drawn machine room contradicts the MRL recommendation (C-05)?
  • Are the per-storey riser heights (C-07) acceptable to the client?
