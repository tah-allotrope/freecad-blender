01 / Executive Summary

Executive Summary

The defect

The render-vs-drawing comparison flagged two departures: the front facade showed no glazing on any storey, and balconies/terraces rendered as sealed rooms. Both traced to one cause — the Blender scene builder built a full-height wall on every balcony edge, and the auto-parapet only added a railing on top of that wall.

The fix

The compiled model now tags each exterior wall with the single room it belongs to (Wall.room_id), and the builder skips any wall owned by a balcony room that carries no opening. The existing parapet code then rails exactly those edges. No spec, no check-registry, and no 2D-plan behaviour changed.

Headline result

Both real designs rebuilt at final quality on legacy EEVEE and republished — contractor-as-drawn (12 views) and tubehouse-dream (9 views). All renders fresh and neutral (no red corruption). Tests went 131 → 134, all passing.

The honest caveat

The fix is verified geometrically (each balcony owns exactly its open edges, and none of those edges carries an opening), not by a pixel test — the Blender side isn't importable under pytest, and this environment cannot display rendered images. The verification is the build completing cleanly plus the ownership/parapet correspondence check.

02 / Background & Objective

Background & Objective

A render-vs-drawing comparison of the contractor's scheme produced findings 05 and 06: the front facade has no visible glazing, and every ban_công / sân thượng zone renders as a closed room. Reading build_scene.py confirmed the cause — build_walls() constructs every wall in the compiled model unconditionally, and _add_balcony_parapets() adds its 1100mm railing alongside that wall rather than in place of it. The objective was to fix the root cause (not paper over it per-design), apply it to both shipped designs, and republish their galleries so the balconies read as open, railed terraces.

04 / Assumptions & Constraints

Assumptions & Constraints

Assumptions

  • No spec authors an opening on a balcony's own free edge; both real designs put doors/windows on the shared partition wall. The suppression check still guards this (condition 3) so a future design loses nothing.
  • A partition wall never needs an owner — suppression only ever applies to a balcony's own free edge, which is by construction an exterior wall.

Constraints

  • Renders must run on Blender 4.1 legacy EEVEE (EEVEE Next renders blood-red on this iGPU).
  • build_scene.py imports bpy and is not pytest-testable — verification is a real build plus geometric checks.
  • Wall is a dataclass round-tripped by asdict()/Wall(**w); the new field is default-valued so old cached .model.json files still load.
05 / Methodology

Methodology

The fix was made at the model schema rather than by recomputing adjacency inside the Blender script. _derive_walls() already computes, then discards, the exact set of room ids (covering) that produced each wall segment; one field (room_id) captures it instead. The builder then applies a three-condition suppression rule — the wall is skipped only when it has an owner, that owner is a balcony room, and the wall carries no opening — and _add_balcony_parapets() continues to rail the same edges via open_edges(). Verification pairs a full homedesign build with a correspondence check that, for every balcony in both designs, the walls it owns equal its open edges and none of those walls carries an opening.

07 / Findings & Recommendation

Findings & Recommendation

Finding

Balconies and terraces rendered as sealed volumes, and the front facade showed no glazing, because a full-height wall was built on every balcony edge with a railing added on top. This affected every design in the repo with a balcony, not just the contractor scheme.

Resolution

Exterior walls now know their owning room, and the builder no longer builds a wall on a balcony's own open edge — the parapet is the only element there. The shared partition wall (which carries the door and window that make the balcony reachable and daylit) is untouched, so the bedroom-to-balcony glazing is now visible from outside.

Recommendation

Ship the republished galleries. Keep the deferred refinement — a short kerb/upstand under the railing — on the backlog for when a review decides the fully-open edge reads wrong; it needs a drawn dimension to source its height from, which neither design provides today.

09 / Risks / Open Questions

Risks / Open Questions

Risks

  • A full-width front balcony also drops its west/east party-wall edges (they are "open" in the model's terms). Accepted for now; a kerb or a party-wall-aware distinction is the future refinement.
  • A future design that authors an opening on a balcony's free edge would silently keep that wall — the suppression rule's condition 3 preserves the opening at the cost of that one edge.

Open questions

  • Does a front balcony in this typology read better with side walls (party) and only a front rail?
  • Is a kerb under the railing warranted for realism in a future pass?
