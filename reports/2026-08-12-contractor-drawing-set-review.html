Checks passed

The geometry is sound. The core stacks exactly on all seven levels, the stair is generously wide, risers are gentle, and the lift shaft is larger than the brief asked for — it resolves the tightest constraint identified in today's lift research. What is not sound is the fit to the brief and the completeness of the package: the mixed-use programme has silently become a single-family house, the light well has been capped with glass, the setback geometry is unresolved against a skewed boundary, and there is no title block on any sheet.

03 Blocking findings

These change the building or stop the permit application. Each carries the evidence it was derived from.

C-01Blocking

The mixed-use programme has become a single-family house

The brief specifies leased commercial space on the ground and first floors — a garage-fronted retail/office bay plus a lease studio — stacked below three private family floors, with a lockable family lobby at Floor 2 restricting the shared stair and lift core to residents beyond that point.

The issued set has none of this. Tầng 1 is garage, living room and kitchen/dining; lửng is a family room; tầng 2–5 are bedrooms with a P.THỜ on tầng 5. There is no lease tenancy, no separate entrance, and no lockable lobby anywhere in the core. Access separation cannot be retrofitted later without re-planning the core.

AskIf the client dropped the lease floors, confirm it in writing and reissue the brief — the current brief and the current drawings describe two different buildings. If they did not, this set needs re-planning, not mark-ups.
Source: brief narrative + requirement 7 · MB 1-LUNG, MB 2-3-4, MB 5-MAI
C-02Blocking

The core light well is capped with glass, defeating the stack effect

The brief's light well is open to the sky from the ground floor to the roof terrace, and the stated reason is ventilation as much as daylight: it "drives stack-effect ventilation, drawing warm air up and out through the open roof void" in a hot-humid climate.

The roof plan labels the 1700 × 1800 mm opening over the core ô kính lấy sáng — a glazed light opening. Glass admits the daylight but closes the exhaust path, which is the half of the well that does the cooling. A second, smaller ô lấy sáng appears to remain open, so some stack path may survive, but the main core void does not.

AskConfirm whether the glazing is fixed or operable, and how the well exhausts. An openable/louvred rooflight or a raised lantern with side vents keeps both the rain exclusion and the stack effect.
Source: brief narrative ¶2 · MB MAI-MD roof plan annotation
C-03Blocking

Orthogonal building against a skewed boundary — rear setback unresolved

Both the road boundary (ranh lộ giới) and the rear boundary run at roughly 7.2° to the party walls, but the building is set out orthogonally. The yards therefore taper: the front yard runs 3000 mm down to 2500 mm, and the rear yard from 2500 mm down to 2000 mm at the east party wall.

On the ground-floor plan the dashed ranh khoảng lùi sau line crosses the building's rear wall around mid-width, so the eastern portion of that wall sits on the wrong side of the drawn setback line — on my measurement by roughly 0.2–0.4 m at the party wall, tapering to compliant at the west. The same crossing occurs at the front, where the setback line passes through the garage door opening.

No dimension anywhere in the set gives the clearance measured perpendicular to the actual boundary at its tightest point, so the drawings cannot be checked against the permit condition as issued.

AskDimension the minimum front and rear clearances perpendicular to the surveyed boundary, and state the required setback. If the rear yard minimum is 2.0 m, the east corner has zero tolerance — a survey error or a site-setting-out error in that direction is an immediate breach.
Source: MB 1-LUNG · boundary slope measured at 0.1255 (7.15°) across the 3950 mm width
C-04Blocking

No title block, no north point, on any sheet

None of the five sheets carries a title block: no project name, address, client, design firm, drawing number, revision, date, scale confirmation, or signature. A permit set (hồ sơ xin phép xây dựng) will not be accepted in this state, and there is no way to tell which revision anyone is holding.

No plan carries a north point either. The brief explicitly flags this as open — "facade orientation is not yet fixed to a real site; confirm solar orientation before finalizing shading design for the street-facing glazing" — and without north on the drawings that question cannot even be asked, let alone closed.

AskReissue with a full title block and revision table on every sheet, and a north point on every plan.
Source: all five sheets · brief requirement 6

04 Important findings

Not permit blockers, but each will cost money or rework if it reaches site unresolved.

C-05Important

The roof plant room contradicts the recommended machine-room-less lift

Today's lift research recommended a traction MRL from a global brand with a Vietnamese entity — KONE MonoSpace Home or Otis Gen2 Life — specifically because the machine sits inside the hoistway and needs no machine room. The contractor has drawn a dedicated ô kỹ thuật thang máy of 2000 × 1900 mm standing 2000 mm above the roof slab, which is what a machine-room lift needs.

Two consequences. If an MRL is selected, that 3.8 m² structure is largely redundant and the building top could come down from +25.800. If it is retained, it must be checked against the permitted height and storey count, because rooftop plant frequently counts toward both.

AskState the intended lift technology on the drawings, then size the overrun to that product rather than to a generic machine room.
Source: MB MAI-MD, MC A-A · research/2026-08-12_tubehouse-lift-comparison.md
C-06Important

No lift pit or overhead dimensioned

The research named per-product pit depth and top-landing overhead as the single biggest unverifiable data gap, and the one thing every supplier quote must state. The section shows neither: there is no pit at the base of the shaft and no overhead dimension at the top landing.

AskAdd pit depth and overhead to the section, taken from the actual product's shaft drawing. This is the item most likely to force a structural change late.
Source: MC A-A · research synthesis, "Before shortlisting, get 3–5 written quotes"
C-07Important

One stair block copy-pasted across three different storey heights

The identical 21-tread stair is drawn on every floor plan, but the storeys it serves are 3800, 3200 and 3400 mm. Those cannot all be climbed by the same 21 treads at the same riser: 22 risers gives 172.7 mm on the ground flight, 145.5 mm at lửng and 154.5 mm on the typical floors.

All three are safely under the 190 mm maximum the pipeline enforces, so nothing here is dangerous — but the set never states which it is, so the builder will decide on site, and a 27 mm riser difference between adjacent flights is exactly the kind of inconsistency that causes trips.

AskIssue a stair setting-out detail per storey: riser count, riser height, going, and total run.
Source: MB 1-LUNG, MB 2-3-4, MB 5-MAI (identical blocks) vs level table on MC A-A
C-08Important

Winder geometry undimensioned at the narrow end

The 180° turn is made with roughly eight winders radiating from the two right-hand corners of the central newel. Treads that fan off a corner taper toward zero going at their inner end. The walking line further out is likely fine, but nothing in the set dimensions either the narrow-end going or the going at the walking line.

The straight treads measure ~250 mm going against a 145–173 mm riser, giving 2R+G ≈ 559 mm — inside the comfortable band but at its lower edge, so the winders have no margin to give away.

AskDimension the winders at the narrow end and at the walking line, and confirm both against the stair standard being applied. This is the most safety-relevant detail in the package.
Source: MB 2-3-4 stair, measured at 16× from the vector geometry
C-09Important

Height and storey count need checking against the local plan

Seven levels plus rooftop plant on a 3.95 m frontage reaches +25.800. Whether that is permitted depends entirely on the lot's road width and the local quy hoạch, which I cannot verify from this package — but on a plot this narrow it is the largest single permit risk in the set, and nothing in the drawings addresses it.

AskConfirm the permitted storey count and height for this lot's road width, in writing, before any structural design proceeds.
Source: MB MAI-MD elevation, MC A-A level table

05 Drawing quality

Reissue items. None changes the building; all of them cost the reader time or invite a measuring error.

C-10Reissue

Sheets are labelled 1:100 but plot at about 1:122

Every sheet carries TL : 1/100. Measured on the issued A4 PDFs, the 3950 mm plot width occupies 91.8 pt and the 20 900 mm depth occupies 485.6 pt — both give ~43.0 mm per point, against 35.28 for a true 1:100. The drawings are internally consistent; it is the paper scale that is wrong, most likely a fit-to-page export from a larger sheet.

AskReissue at true scale, or supply the DWG. Until then nobody should scale off these PDFs — a measured dimension will read about 18% short.
Source: two independent calibrations, MB 1-LUNG and MB 2-3-4
C-11Reissue

Section A-A is plotted rotated 90°

The section sheet reads sideways in every PDF viewer — the title block text and all level tags run vertically. It has to be rotated by hand before it can be read or marked up.

Source: MC A-A
C-12Reissue

Only one section and one elevation

The set has a single long section (A-A) and the front elevation. For a seven-level house on a skewed plot there is no rear elevation and no cross-section, so the lô gia and rear yard relationships, and the light-well edges, are never drawn in the vertical plane.

AskAdd a cross-section through the core and light well, and the rear elevation.
Source: sheet register
C-13Reissue

Brief items not covered by this package

Three brief requirements have no counterpart in the set: light-well drainage at its ground-floor base and waterproofing at every floor edge abutting it; a vertical services riser for electrical, plumbing and data alongside the core, accessible from each floor; and condensation-controlled operable glazing where rooms face the well. These normally live in later detail packages, so this is a coverage note rather than a defect — but the services riser in particular affects the core plan and should be located now, not later.

Source: brief requirements 2, 3, 5

07 One thing to update on our side

The lift research finished this morning was written against a 1000 × 1400 mm (1.4 m²) shaft taken from designs/tubehouse-dream.json, and its central conclusion was that space is the deciding constraint — that the shaft sat at the lower bound of Vietnam's home-lift segment, below one installer's ~1.5 m² practical minimum.

The contractor's shaft is ~1500 × 1600 mm (2.4 m²). That premise no longer holds: the tightest constraint in the study is relieved, the viable product list widens, and the local-assembled traction tier becomes a genuine option rather than a squeeze. The stop count also changed — the brief's five levels are seven stops here, including the roof terrace. Both belong in a revision to that brief before it is used to solicit quotes.
